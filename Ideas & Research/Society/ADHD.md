Last week, I formally tested for ADHD, and today, I was diagnosed \*not\* to have it.

Why?

Because I was “too smart” and “too engaged” during the evaluation. It’s ironic—what holds my attention is something that truly captivates or challenges me. But if it’s mundane, routine, or uninteresting, my focus evaporates entirely.

Despite everything I described about my struggles, I was told that a negative test result makes it illegal to prescribe ADHD medication.

My Reality Without ADHD Medication:

\- I consume an eighth to a quarter of weed daily, plus half a gram to a gram of wax, and at least half a gram of cart.

\- Marijuana helps me stay productive, but only when the task is engaging and exciting—like music or writing.

\- Without medication, after completing those tasks, I crash completely. I lack the energy or drive to do \*anything\* else.

My Reality With ADHD Medication:

\- My marijuana consumption drops by about 70%.

\- I’m able to focus on tasks—even the boring, unengaging ones—and follow through.

\- I’m able to leave my house, stay out, and work productively for 6+ hours.

\- My motivation to plan for the future, such as looking into music or writing classes, increases.

\---

The disconnect lies in how ADHD manifests. The psychiatrist dismissed the possibility of ADHD because I was “engaged” during the test, but that’s the very nature of ADHD—it’s not a lack of attention but the inability to regulate it.

What makes this harder is how my neurodivergence intersects with trauma, anxiety, and depression.

Diagnosed Conditions:

\- Major Depressive Disorder: Non-responsive to antidepressants; they make it worse.

\- Extreme Anxiety Disorder: This includes xenophobia and constant overwhelm.

\- PTSD: Deeply tied to my lived experiences, further complicating my focus and productivity.

\- PDA: Pathological Demand Avoidance. Whenever I'm given a “demand” by myself or another person. I avoid it.

\---

The result? A system that effectively tells me: “You’re on your own.”

Even with my benzodiazepines, I feel lost and overwhelmed most of the time—trapped in my room, drowning in my own thoughts. Without ADHD medication, basic tasks feel insurmountable. I struggle to leave the house for more than 2 hours.

When I \*have\* the right medication, I can focus, engage, and thrive. I can work. I can stay out for hours. I can even think about pursuing music and writing classes—dreams that feel impossible otherwise.

Instead, I’m prescribed Strattera, which my other doctor wouldn’t even consider. I’ll try it, but I’m not optimistic. It feels like I’m stuck in a draconian system that prioritizes rules over genuine help.

\---

This isn’t just a problem with me. It’s a problem with how the system evaluates and treats neurodivergence. It doesn’t account for the nuanced ways conditions manifest or overlap.

I’m tired of being told what I \*don’t\* have without being given effective solutions for what I \*do\* experience.
