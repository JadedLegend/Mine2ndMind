Hey, did I ever tell you guys

The story 'bout when I was 25?

In New Mexico, trying to find a flow

With my father—well, I never found it though.

But nonetheless, surrounded by these ancient,

Beautiful mountains, I felt something sacred.

My stepmother, she gave me something unexpected,

An experience that shaped who I am, left me affected.

She learned, through her therapy,

That she could breathe—holotropically.

Until these other entities entered her mind,

Through her breathwork, crossing dimensions, space, and time.

Marianne even charged others a fee

For a session, so they could come and ask

Their burning questions to these beings through her face.

They said their names were John and Grace.

John said he was the Baptist—yes, \*the\* disciple.

And Grace, a young soul, so thrilled to meet Michael.

I’m like, "Alright, it’s nice to meet you too,"

But damn, it felt like my brain rang true.

It was such a strange thing,

Watching her shaking, adjusting the frequency,

Her voice changing completely—

Between a man’s tone and a woman’s.

I’ve played it back in my mind a thousand times,

Was left with no choice but to wonder why.

It was far above my head to fully comprehend,

But man, I wish I had those tapes again.

I’d sample that shit—bet.

Somewhere, on an old laptop, traces remain.

They told me they were excited to meet me.

Thirty minutes in, answering all my ignorant questions,

Grace blurts out, “I admire you, Great Archangel Michael.”

Yo, what? That left me spinning in all directions.

That led to a conversation I can barely recall.

All I know is that it blew my mind to bits,

Made me feel a little psycho, like,

“Yo, is this even real life though?”

After researching like a lunatic,

I pieced together a story that fit—

For me, at least.

But even now, it sits at the edge of my mind,

Where all I could ever find was speculation.

I remember coming down the stairs,

Seeing my dad there, tears in his eyes.

For once, he let his blank stare crack a little.

My stepmother says, “Bill, you have an amazing son.”

He chuckles, “Yeah, I know.”

She says, “No, really.”

But he never understood. He never tried to know me.

And that left a scar so deep, it still shows, see?

How could the Archangel Michael hate his father?

That thought's burned into my soul,

Forsaken from a young age, a story untold.

Scars and trauma, mistakes I’ve made—

Pissing in sacred waters,

Defiling the very things I prayed to.

And honestly?

It’s ridiculous to think some divine intervention

Could be tied to this chaotic dimension.

Even when I’m deep under certain tryptamines,

I’ve got no clue what anything really means.

It’s just me. Alone in this.

No one to trust but me.

Even my own eyes can lie to me—

The world bends, twists, and cheats.

Where’s this supposed guidance when I’m driven

Straight into madness and insanity?

I don’t recall signing up for this job,

But apparently, it’s my destiny?

Always addicted to something—

Sometimes sugar, sometimes pills.

But I’m weak.

Can barely eat, can barely sleep,

A walking shell so easily defeated.

Still, I feel it.

And yet, I say, "Fuck it."

Maybe I’ll have another chance—

In another 8 billion years.
