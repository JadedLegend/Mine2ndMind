Lately, every time I put on music, I find myself reaching for Koresma. Man, dude’s a legend. Some of my favorite songs have been crafted around his soundscapes. His music feels like a canvas that I can build on—a foundation that invites something new to emerge.

As always, I reach out to the producers whose tracks I essentially take and infringe on, turning their creations into something entirely my own. Most times, I get silence, which I’ve learned to expect. But occasionally, I’ll hear back—like Donkeychote about a month ago. He was one of only two producers who’ve ever asked me to take my song down. Sure, whatever, dude. Go get your money or whatever validation you’re chasing.

But back to the point: I’ve sent countless emails and messages to Koresma, Emancipator, PantyRaid, Tipper—some of the artists whose work I’ve been most inspired by. And nothing. Not a word. Do I take it personally? Honestly, yeah. I take everything personally. But I also try to stay aware of the fact that every person is living out their own unique life, navigating challenges and experiences I’ll never fully know. My belief is that everyone does the best they can with the tools they’ve been given—or found.

What culture tells me about art, though, often clashes with my own guiding beliefs. I see art as something divine, something that transcends the individual. Sure, people spend years honing their skills, but the inspiration? The spark? That comes from somewhere far beyond us.

Art and money, though—they just don’t mix. It’s painful to see a genius musical artist struggling to survive, working two side jobs just to make ends meet, while someone else pins a banana to a wall and sells it for $120,000. Moments like that make me question everything: the value of money, the system we’ve built, and the audacity of humanity to call itself \*sapiens\*. Intelligent? Maybe. Wise? Sometimes I’m not so sure.

But what do I know? Nothing, apparently. And people are quick to remind me of that. It feels like everyone’s eager to weaponize my past against me or, more often, just ignore me entirely.

My life has felt like a series of those moments. I was bossed around, told what to do, and fed a narrative that never sat right with me. I saw through it early on and decided to stop eating the shit being served. Instead, I started creating my own sustenance—building my own ideas, shaping my own experiments with this life.

And that’s what this is to me: an experiment. This life, this fleeting moment, this tiny speck of sand—I don’t weigh it down with unnecessary purpose or illusions of grandeur. I have grand illusions, sure, but they rarely revolve around the me that exists right now. This "me" is just the current lens, the temporary avatar.

In the end, I figured it out: everything is nothing. And somehow, that’s everything.
