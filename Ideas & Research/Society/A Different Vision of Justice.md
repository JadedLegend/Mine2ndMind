**A Different Perspective on Justice**

What would it mean to create a justice system not rooted in vengeance or profit, but in remembrance?

To remember that every human being is more than their worst decision. That pain breeds pain, and healing breeds something else - humility, perhaps. Wisdom. The kind you can’t legislate, only live.

I’m not naïve. Some actions require removal. Some behaviors endanger others. But we can hold accountability without abandoning compassion. We can build systems that help people return to themselves, instead of forcing them deeper into despair.

**The Real Injustice**

There’s something obscene about a culture that spends more on cages than classrooms and rewards those who exploit the vulnerable.

We normalize this madness by just calling it “normal.” We scale monuments. Places about 40? We tell ourselves the system’s too complex to change, but it was designed—crafted intentionally—and anything designed can be destroyed, rearranged, built again.

The justice system doesn’t fail. It feeds, on fear, on power, on our silence.

III. Justice as Resonance, Not Revenge

What if justice isn’t about retribution?

What if it’s about resonance?

Like a tuning fork, humming back into coherence.

What if our systems existed not to dominate, but to restore?

What is truly righteous and just? And who do we trust and employ in order to uphold this cornerstone of society?

IV. From Structural Rot to Structural Repair

We need something more radical than reform. Reform implies patching a broken machine. But what if the machine was built to break us?

This isn’t about political parties. It’s not red or blue. It’s structural rot, systemic decay.

We don’t need better rules for the same game.

We need a new game entirely.

One based in care, truth, and reciprocity.

One that sees people, not products.

One that knows a healed society is safer than a surveilled one.

V. Final Reflection: Build the Bridge

If justice is to mean anything again, it must become something more than the sum of its laws. It must become a living process. Something we shape together.

Let it be a bridge:

from harm to healing,

from punishment to possibility,

from silence to understanding.

Let justice evolve,

as we evolve.

And may we finally remember.

Truth and justice aren’t things we find.

They’re things we build.

Together.
