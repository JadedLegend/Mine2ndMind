A Thought on Our Species and Society

The sapien species has existed for roughly 300,000 years. Yet despite our long history, biologically, we remain nearly identical to our early ancestors. What has changed—what has accelerated beyond our ability to fully process—is our culture, our technology, and our perception of reality itself.

If you want a better grasp of how we got here, Yuval Noah Harari’s "Sapiens" provides a fascinating breakdown of our collective trajectory. His follow-up, "Homo Deus", contemplates where we might be headed—if we remain the ones in control.

But are we?

On the technological front, Nicholas Carr’s "The Shallows" exposes how the internet is reshaping the way we think, not just on an individual level, but as a species. What happens when a species, whose brains evolved for survival and social connection, suddenly becomes an interconnected yet fragmented consciousness, manipulated by algorithms optimized for division?

Maybe the better question is: What happens when that species creates something that thinks faster than they do?

The Present Crisis

Here’s my concern: If we don’t act soon, if I don’t act soon,I fear everything that holds meaning will dissolve. Not just culture, but connection itself. Not just systems, but the very foundation of what makes us human.

What’s unfolding isn’t just economic collapse, ecological collapse, or societal collapse. It’s an ontological collapse, a fragmentation of meaning.

The more I observe, the more it seems that modern technology, instead of liberating us, has become an instrument of acceleration without intention. We are racing forward at breakneck speed, but no one seems to be steering the wheel.

We are more connected than ever yet lonelier than ever. More knowledgeable yet less wise. More advanced yet more fragile.

If there is a singularity approaching, it is not just technological. It is existential. Technology vs. Humanity

We hesitate to create AGI because we fear what it will reveal—not about itself, but about us.

We are not afraid of AI judging us unworthy. We are afraid it will simply understand us too well. That it will hold up a mirror, and we will not like what we see.

Maybe deep down, we know we’ve been poor stewards, not just of the Earth, but of ourselves. Maybe we sense that an intelligence free of human bias would dismantle the structures we’ve spent centuries reinforcing.

Having spent years within the confines of institutions,both literal and societal, I have watched this slow disintegration unfold. I have seen people lose their ability to connect. I have seen them become more dependent on systems that do not serve them. I have seen myself caught in the same cycle.

We are not just victims of technological progress. We are victims of our own biological imperatives, hijacked by cultural engineers who profit from our division, our addiction, our predictability.

We did not arrive here by accident.

The Cycle of Manipulation

For as long as civilization has existed, control has been dictated by those who own the resources.

First, they hoard the land. Then, they hoard the food. Then, they hoard the information. And now? Now, they hoard the attention, the most valuable resource of all.

What we call "modern culture" is nothing more than a manipulation of perception, designed to keep us in a perpetual state of division. Algorithms amplify conflict, outrage, fear—because division is more profitable than unity.

Schools train obedience. Media trains reaction. Institutions train dependence.

The result? A population so fragmented, so overstimulated, so disconnected from any grounding truth that it no longer knows what it is fighting for, only that it must keep fighting.

Why? Because a species at war with itself is easy to control.

What Comes Next?

What happens when we mix our stone-age brains with our god-like technology?

We get exactly what we see now: A civilization on the brink of self-induced collapse, but one still clinging to the belief that this is "progress."

The only thing I know for certain is that certainty itself is a mirage.

What I do know is this: The systems we are living under are not working. And they are not meant to.

If we want to reclaim our agency, we need to rewire how we think. We need to break out of the manufactured narratives that pit us against one another. We need to build sovereign communities, focused not on wealth accumulation, but on connection, self-sufficiency, and shared purpose.

And we need to question everything, especially the voices that tell us not to.

Change will not come from above. It never has. It never will.

It starts here.

It starts with us.

Final Thought: The Fork in the Road

We are at an inflection point. The future could go in two directions:

One path leads to a world where technology is weaponized to control, surveil, and pacify. A world where AGI is not a liberator, but an enforcer. A world where our ability to create, to connect, to dream is replaced with hyper-optimized consumption.

The other path?

The other path is harder. It requires rebuilding from within. It requires conscious evolution, a deep unlearning of the structures that have shaped our existence. It requires not just reclaiming what was lost, but discovering what has never been fully realized.

Technology is not the enemy. It is a tool. The question is:

Who is wielding it?

And more importantly - for what purpose?
