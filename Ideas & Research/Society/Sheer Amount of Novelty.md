The sheer amount of novelty on Amazon is truly unbelievable. We've come so far. The fact that I can buy something with a click and have it at my door, sometimes on the same day, truly blows my mind. It’s the kind of thing that would astound our ancestors.

What happens when we advance even further? When we can 3D print anything we need—or summon and teleport it instantly?

Amazon, despite its convenience, operates on a system eerily similar to slavery—just with extra layers of corporate protection. It’s more like indentured servitude, where no matter how hard you work, you never really own anything, nor do you have a realistic chance to escape the system. Yet they dangle the illusion of “opportunity” in front of us like a carrot on a stick. How did we let it get to this point?

Economics, as a system, wasn’t designed to serve everyone. It’s a tool to divide us into “haves” and “have-nots.”

In America, at least, it seems that the “forgotten generation”—those who were our age during World War II—played a pivotal role in shaping this outcome. When they entered positions of power in the 1960s and 1970s, they passed legislation that gave corporations greater rights than humans in many cases. The logic was simple but flawed: groups of people (corporations) would naturally act for the greater good. Spoiler: they didn’t.

They also detached the American dollar from the gold standard, turning it into fiat currency—backed by nothing. Money became just numbers in a server or slips of paper with no intrinsic value.

At the same time, the era of Watergate, Reaganomics, and foreign interventions like the Contras unfolded, exposing the deep corruption in both corporate and governmental systems. Yet nothing really changed. People didn’t seem to care—just like now.

The result? A few corporations have taken over \*everything\*. Companies like BlackRock, Vanguard, Google, and Amazon dominate our lives. Capitalist consumerism, long outgrown as a viable system, still rules us because we’re too dependent on it—and on them. They know it, and they exploit it.

A century ago, wealth was distributed more equally. Sure, the system was still controlled, but at least individuals had a chance. You could work hard, open a small business, and build something for yourself. Now, small businesses are devoured by mega-corporations.

This isn’t some anomaly; it’s how capitalism naturally evolves. A system based on exploitation inevitably consumes everything in its path. It’s no surprise that capitalism was built on the backs of slaves, then indentured laborers, and later low-wage workers exploited under the guise of “opportunity.”

The infrastructure of America was literally built by marginalized groups: slaves, Chinese immigrants working for pennies on the railroads, and other exploited laborers. Yet the narrative remains: “Work hard, and you can achieve anything.” For most people, that’s no longer true.

Today, the system is stable enough—and resources are abundant enough—that everyone could easily have the basics: food, water, shelter, and security. But instead of sharing, the few hoard while the many struggle.

If you’re one of the few who’ve gamed the system and found financial stability, congratulations. But let’s be honest: most people who achieve wealth either inherit it or exploit others to get there. Those who truly “build from nothing” are rare—and even they live with the constant fear of losing it all.

The statistics are grim. Did you know 90% of Americans earn less than $32,000 a year? Meanwhile, a tiny elite flaunts their wealth in our faces, spending fortunes on frivolous luxuries while millions starve. It’s sickening to think how many lives could be saved with just a fraction of that wealth.

Money, once a simple tool for barter, has become a weapon of control. Those who hold the most use it to manipulate society. It’s no coincidence that corporations have more rights than individuals. The system was designed that way.

Here we are in 2024, with technology and science advanced enough to make work unnecessary for survival. We could be living in a society where people pursue their passions instead of toiling away for a paycheck. But instead, we’re trapped in a system where work is mandatory, even when it’s pointless.

What if wealth was measured differently? Not in dollars, but in health, community, self-fulfillment, and purpose? We’ve never raised a generation like that, but imagine what the world could be if we did.

The earth, despite our abuse, still has plenty to give. Like us, it has the ability to heal and thrive if given the right conditions. But we’ve foolishly acted as if we’re smarter than nature, and the consequences are catching up to us. Microplastics, radiation, climate change—these are just some of the ways we’ve poisoned our planet and ourselves.

Yet, instead of addressing these crises, the system doubles down. They force us to work, scare us with manufactured shortages, and keep us distracted with endless entertainment. It’s all designed to maintain control. If you look closely, you’ll see the truth: the narrative is manipulated, and money is the puppeteer.

The resources exist to ensure everyone has enough, but greed prevents it. Empty foreclosed homes sit unused while people sleep on the streets. Basic needs like healthcare and clean water are treated as privileges instead of rights.

Universal Basic Income might be a bandaid solution in the coming years, but it won’t address the root problem: a system built on exploitation can never be truly fair.

I’m not here to redefine economics or fix the world. I’m just one voice, venting about a broken system. Hard work is admirable, but it’s just a trait—not a guarantee of success. Everyone’s reality is different, and society doesn’t make room for those who don’t fit its mold.

It’s time to rethink everything. Because the system we’re clinging to? It’s already failed us.
