I find it baffling how many people claim to be spiritual "experts," acting as though they hold the keys to the universe while knowing an infinitesimal fraction of what’s truly out there—maybe 0.00000001%. And yet, these individuals often attract thousands of loyal followers, many of whom need an editor far more desperately than I do.

This might be one of my biggest challenges: my awareness. I’ve often said it’s far more intelligent to admit you know nothing than to act like an expert based on limited experience.

Even if you’ve spent 10,000 hours mastering something, you must remain open to the idea that what you’ve learned—what you think you "know"—could be incomplete or even entirely false. That’s just reality.

Words themselves are just symbols, attempts to point the way. But I’ve come to believe they’re interpreted differently by everyone, shaped by individual experience and perception. They can never fully capture the ineffable nature of direct experience.

Because direct experience? It’s always moving, always shifting. It can’t be pinned down or fully explained. Words are like trying to catch water in your hands—they hold some, but most slips through.

You can’t always trust your senses to tell you the truth. You certainly can’t trust everything you read, and the more I observe, the clearer it becomes: people gravitate toward those who claim certainty, those who act as if their theories are facts.

Why? Because people want reassurance. They want an authoritative figure to validate their thoughts, to tell them they’re "right." And in that reassurance, they hand over their curiosity, their doubt, their openness to new perspectives.

But "right" and "wrong"? "Good" and "bad"? These are all human constructs, designed not to understand the world but to divide it—and us.

If things were allowed to simply \*be\*, without the constant push of malicious manipulation, humanity would be unrecognizable. Better, I think. Different.

I try to hold my foundational beliefs lightly. I know they could give out at any second. I don’t cling to them for dear life—I stay open, ready to adjust, to learn, to let go. All I ask is proof.

But even then, truth is rarely absolute. We live on Earth, each of us carrying our own subjective experience, shaped by our own lenses. What’s true for one person might not be true for another—and that’s okay.

Some things might be harmful to 80% of people while beneficial to the other 20%. Those are random numbers, of course, but they illustrate the point: truth is personal, fluid, and ever-changing. To act like it’s anything else is to miss the point entirely. .
