It's remarkable how you can \*never really\* know someone. There's never a true chance to steal a glance of another’s perspective. All you have is the story and picture you’ve built of them—and people change. Some hide their true intentions incredibly well. The deceit and trickery in our reality are staggering, at least to me.

In the end, it’s just you. From the womb to the tomb.

What baffles me is how most people don’t see this. Truth, to me, feels like the lighter, easier way through life. But I think I understand why it’s not embraced by all. I believe backstabbing and separation from fellow humans are subconsciously programmed into us—reinforced by cultural engineers.

It’s hard not to notice that most people look out for #1, and #1 alone. I’ve never understood it. Sure, I’ve been fortunate to have support for much of my life. But even with that, there have been dozens of times I’ve gone without—just to make someone else’s life a little, or sometimes a lot, easier. I’ve gone without food. I’ve spent cold nights. I’ve given away my last $5, my last cigarette. I even shared with addicts when they were sick, knowing full well I’d fall back into hoarding mode once the moment passed.

Is my situation different because I trust I’ll always be provided for?

No. Because I don’t know that. I’m painfully aware that everything could change in an instant. Every day, the world teeters on uncertainty.

I don’t think I could navigate this society alone—not with the tools and information I have right now. Without the singular person who helps me, I’d have likely been mentally broken down years ago, living under a bridge. That one person gives me a glimmer of hope—a belief that, maybe, just maybe, humanity is more good than evil.

"Good" and "evil" are subjective, I know. But there are universal concepts like pleasant and unpleasant. And I prefer pleasant—that’s basically my life’s goal.

I’ve let people borrow my car for months, multiple times, while they tried to piece their lives back together. I’ve put my life on hold to help others stabilize theirs. And what do I get in return? Usually, I get walked on and taken advantage of. Lied to and manipulated for more.

Don’t mistake my kindness for weakness or ignorance. I treat others how I want to be treated—until they show me who they are. Then I mirror it back.

But smart narcissists? They’re my Achilles heel. They do just enough visible good to mask their real intentions, and I keep coming back, hoping the world won’t lose one of the "good ones."

In my entire life, I can count on two hands—maybe even one—the times I’ve felt genuine warmth and hope from another human in a moment of dire need. Stacey. Jonathan. Aaron. And maybe a handful of random strangers I never saw again.

Far more often, I’ve been in desperate need, only to be met with indifference. Strangers honking, laughing, throwing things instead of stopping to ask if I’m okay.

It’s metaphorical, sure, but it’s also literal. When I was 14, I had a horrible four-wheeler accident—broke both wrists, dislocated growth plates, shattered my face and jaw. I was alone in the woods and had to walk miles to get help. Cars passed me. I remember a truck full of people pointing and laughing as they drove by. Not one person stopped to help. It wasn’t until my dad, who had been searching for me, found me that I finally got to the hospital.

I’ve been at the ankles of people I’ve helped, begging for aid, and they’ve walked away. Time and time again, it’s the same story.

A part of me still holds out hope, thinking maybe there are good people out there—protected, insulated, keeping the ignorant at bay. Maybe that’s why they’re so hard to find. Or more likely, they're drowning in society's normal sea; of nonsensical and nonserving, responsibility and time wasting.

I’ve been searching for where I fit for a decade. Every puzzle I’ve tried to fit into so far has rejected me. I don’t know if I’ll ever find what I’m looking for.

In all honesty, I know cosmically, it doesn’t matter. But still… my hope for humanity is hanging by a thread.
