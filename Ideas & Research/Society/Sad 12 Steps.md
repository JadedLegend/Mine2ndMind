The \*really\* sad part about 12-step programs is that, in reality, they often do more harm than good. They’re a masterclass in brainwashing. I’ve participated in Scientology TRs before, and the similarities between those and 12-step meetings are chilling.

Think about it:

\- Saying you’re powerless every day.

\- Repeatedly identifying yourself as an addict or alcoholic.

\- Hearing over and over, “If you smoke weed, you’ll end up in the gutter with a needle in your arm.”

That kind of messaging isn’t recovery—it’s manipulation.

The last time I relapsed, it wasn’t because I wanted to. It was because I had been programmed to believe that if I smoked weed, the next logical step was heroin. That’s not cause and effect; that’s someone else’s narrative hijacking my rational thought.

I needed release. Meditation didn’t work. Writing didn’t work. Talking to people didn’t work. So I turned to weed—and immediately, my brain went, “Might as well go all the way now.”

It’s criminal what these programs do to people. I wish there was some legal precedent to shut them down entirely.

The truth is, anything we hear repeatedly changes our brain. That’s basic neuroscience. And yet, we’ve let 12-step programs dominate the recovery space despite their glaring failures.

Did you know?

\- AA has a 5% retention rate for 1 year success. It has a 0.005% success rate for 15 years.

\- Meanwhile, Yale and Harvard studies show that around 20% of people struggling with addiction spontaneously recover without any program at all.

Let that sink in: the odds of recovery are four times better without AA.
