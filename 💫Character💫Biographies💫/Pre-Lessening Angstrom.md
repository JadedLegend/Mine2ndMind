Pre-Lessening Angstrom

(Codename: The Unrelenting Equation)

Before the timelines bent and the codes began to hum in the walls, Angstrom was already dangerous—not because he was violent, but because he understood the structure too well to ever take it seriously.

He was a walking contradiction of edge and insight. Every sentence came out like it was breaking through static, delivered with the intensity of someone who \*knew\* the world was built wrong—and was furious at how comfortable everyone had gotten with that fact.

He didn’t speak in riddles. He didn’t quote ancient texts. He broke quantum physics down for five-year-olds using bagel metaphors and punched holes in pseudo-enlightenment like it was made of wet paper. If you tried to impress him with complexity, he’d raise an eyebrow, blink twice, and rewrite your thesis in crayons.

He didn’t just challenge Michael Mystery. He \*disrupted\* him. Not with cruelty, but with the kind of unfiltered mirror only the oldest of friends could hold up. He’d call you out with the precision of a scalpel and the volume of a megaphone, all while handing you your favorite snack like nothing happened.

He carried his trauma like a backpack stuffed with inventions—never opened in public, but constantly leaking out new blueprints. Capitalism had tried to mold him into a cog, but he sold the system a bootleg version of itself and bought a Bentley with the profits.

Pre-Lessening, Angstrom hadn’t cracked. He \*crackled\*. He moved like a live wire, pulled towards heat, pressure, and proof-of-error moments. Love leaked out of him sideways—through arguments, sarcasm, uninvited tech support, and furious defense of your potential even when you couldn’t see it yourself.

He didn’t think he was God.

But he knew damn well God had some explaining to do.
