The Role of Art in Humanity’s Survival

Response To Alex Grey's Thoughts

"If we didn't have art—our uncensored and free creative outlet—or didn’t enjoy other people's music, dance, theater, sculpture, painting, and cinema, the world would be filled with far more sickness and hate. A world without art would leave us bereft of a language of beauty, stripping us of one of the primary ways that spirit reaches people."

This thought lingers in my mind, tugging at something I’ve always felt but couldn’t fully articulate. Art is our lifeline to something greater, something transcendent. Yet, even as I hold onto that belief, I can’t ignore a creeping doubt about the future of art itself.

The writer continues, offering what might be a hopeful truth:

"There will always be art. Fortunately, as long as humans exist, a world without art cannot occur, because where there are people, there is art."

But is that entirely true? While I want to believe it, I can’t help but wonder if the art we’re left with will slowly lose its soul. Over the next decade or so, I wouldn’t be surprised if the art we’re allowed to see and experience becomes sanitized, commodified, and disconnected from the raw, unfiltered spirit that makes it meaningful.

Creating in Silence

This hits home for me. I’ve already created so many pieces of art that feel deeply connected to spirit. Music, writing, ideas—they’re not just creations but expressions of something I can barely put into words. Yet no one listens. No one reads. No one cares.

It feels like the art I pour myself into is for me and me alone. And perhaps that’s the point—it’s my therapy, my way of easing the tension in my shoulders, neck, and back where my anxiety sits like a physical weight. But it’s a lonely process.

The shitty part? I know I can’t keep going like this. I can’t keep creating with zero fans and zero interest. Art may come from within, but without connection, without others to share it with, it feels incomplete. It’s not validation I’m looking for—it’s resonance, a moment where someone says, “I see what you’re trying to say.”

A Quiet Battle

Art keeps the spirit alive in a world that often feels determined to crush it. It’s a quiet rebellion, a way of fighting back against the cynicism, the commodification, and the apathy that surrounds us.

But the fight gets harder when it feels like no one’s listening. I know I’m not alone in this struggle—there are countless creators pouring themselves into their work, hoping someone will notice. And maybe that’s why art will never truly disappear. Because as long as humans exist, there will always be people like me, creating not because they want to, but because they \*have\* to.
