What Is Justice? Who Decides What Is True and Just?

Justice has been a cornerstone of human civilization since its earliest days. Ancient societies each developed their own criminal justice systems, but the Babylonians were the first to produce written criminal laws. Their punishments, however, were brutally severe by today’s standards. For instance, if a son struck his father, his hands would be cut off.

Over time, justice has evolved into the system we see today. English common law introduced the concept of evolving interpretation, relying on legal precedents to shape future rulings. The Magna Carta limited the king’s power and established basic rights for citizens, a foundational moment for constitutionalism. Later, the U.S. Constitution enshrined individual rights and created a framework for law that courts continue to interpret.

But this leads to a critical question: Who is fit to navigate this ever-evolving legal maze and decide what is truly just?

The Flaws of the Current System

In the U.S., federal judges are appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate, with no formal constitutional requirements for their qualifications. While this may sound ideal in theory, in practice, the system is riddled with bias and inefficiencies. It has strayed far from its original purpose: to bring order to chaos.

Today, the U.S. incarcerates roughly 2 million people at any given time, the highest rate in the developed world. Many of these individuals are trapped in a system that profits from their misery, thanks to the rise of for-profit prisons in the 1980s and the so-called "war on drugs." This system not only exploits human suffering but also perpetuates cycles of poverty and crime.

I have experienced this system firsthand. During my incarceration, there was no genuine effort to rehabilitate me or others. Instead, I felt like a cog in a machine designed to dehumanize. While I deserved to face consequences for my actions, I met countless others serving longer sentences for minor drug offenses or probation violations. Many were victims of systemic failures, not hardened criminals.

A System Corrupted by Greed

The justice system has become a tool for the powerful to maintain their status. Wealthy corporations and individuals manipulate it to protect their interests, while those without resources face disproportionate consequences. Judges and prosecutors are often influenced by political connections or corporate agendas, undermining the very notion of impartiality.

For every systemic corruption case that comes to light, countless others remain hidden. The system’s complexity shields those who abuse it, allowing them to evade accountability. Meanwhile, the prison system fills with individuals who pose no real threat to society—people struggling with addiction, poverty, or mental health issues.

The War on Drugs and Missed Opportunities

Drug use and addiction are often leveraged as scapegoats for societal problems. Instead of addressing the root causes, we criminalize addiction, pushing millions into the justice system. If drugs were legalized and resources were allocated to treatment and harm reduction, we could dismantle criminal enterprises and begin healing society.

But the justice system resists change. It clings to outdated notions of punishment, ignoring the grey areas of morality and the potential for redemption. Who decides what’s right and wrong for society as a whole? Shouldn’t laws aim to foster love, support, and community rather than division and despair?

Capitalism and the Hijacking of Justice

Our justice system, like human consciousness itself, has been hijacked. The wealthy and powerful feed just enough success stories into the system to maintain the illusion of fairness. Meanwhile, they exploit humanity for personal gain, treating people as resources to manipulate.

The result is a society where abusive prison guards and corrupt police officers operate with impunity, while everyday citizens suffer. Though not all in law enforcement are bad, the significant number of those who abuse their positions warrants scrutiny.

A Call for Reform

Our justice system has become overly complicated, losing sight of its original purpose: to mediate conflicts and bring order. It needs a complete overhaul. Simplify the laws. Remove unnecessary noise. Focus on principles that foster kindness, respect, and accountability.

Imagine a system where rehabilitation replaces punishment, where laws address harm rather than control. Legalizing drugs and investing in effective rehabilitation programs could drastically reduce crime and create space for healing. Simplified laws—“Don’t hurt, don’t steal, don’t exploit”—could guide us toward a more just society.

Most people in prison are not irredeemable. With proper support and therapy, many could become productive members of society. I’ve met individuals in prison with the potential to solve major societal issues, yet they were trapped by circumstances and systemic failures.

The True Injustice

While innocent people languish in prison, those who profit from mass incarceration and disinformation continue to operate freely. The wealthy elite, who exploit humanity and the planet for their gain, are rarely held accountable. The laws that should protect us from them instead shield them from us.

Laws like those banning abortion, even in cases of rape, reveal how unjust our system has become. Such legislation strips individuals of autonomy and dignity, perpetuating division and suffering.

Moving Forward

Our justice system, like so many others, is designed to keep us divided and distracted while the few who wield power move freely. But the cracks are showing. The time has come to reimagine justice—not as a tool of punishment but as a means of fostering unity and fairness.

Let’s focus on rebuilding a system that serves humanity as a whole, not the interests of a select few. Let’s create a culture of compassion, understanding, and mutual respect. Only then can we move beyond the chaos and division that have plagued us for so long.
