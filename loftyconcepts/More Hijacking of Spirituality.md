The Hijacking of Spirituality

It’s common to hear people talk about “seeking spiritual answers” or “finding a path,” but what I’ve witnessed—time and time again—has been both enlightening and disheartening.

When I’ve gone searching for truth, I’ve found countless forms of spirituality, practices, and even direct tools like entheogens. These sacred substances and rituals hold immense potential; I’ve seen them transform lives and crack open new perspectives.

But here’s the problem: these profound experiences—ineffable by nature—are too often reduced to clichés and platitudes. Yes, these sayings may hold fragments of truth, but they also serve as distractions from the deeper work.

Our society has a knack for commodifying everything, even the sacred. Spirituality and sacred medicines are no exception.

We live in a \*globalist technocratic plutocracy\*, a system where capitalism—essentially a more refined form of slavery—dictates the rules. And while many people recognize this, most still play by those same rules, unable to escape the system’s grip.

This is why we see “money-hungry spiritual leaders” and “holistic health centers” that might genuinely want to help but are trapped by the same capitalist framework. Capitalism thrives on competition and commodification, and these forces often stand in direct opposition to individuality and true healing.

The real teachers? The real healers? They exist, but they’re harder to find. They’re buried beneath the noise of algorithms—another tool born from the capitalist machine. These algorithms prioritize profit over authenticity, promoting what sells rather than what heals.

So, while spirituality and entheogens have the power to liberate, they’ve been hijacked by a system designed to commodify even our most sacred experiences. The challenge is to look past the surface, to dig deeper, and to question the systems that frame how we engage with spirituality in the first place.

True healing and understanding won’t come from what’s trending on a feed. It will come from stepping outside the system, finding the teachers who prioritize integrity over profit, and reclaiming our connection to the ineffable.
