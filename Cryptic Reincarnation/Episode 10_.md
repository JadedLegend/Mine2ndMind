Episode 10

Michael's hands sweated, his mouth moving on autopilot while his mind retreated inward. He'd always been socially awkward. Or rather—he'd always been aware that his comfortable, familiar relationship with silence often made social settings uncomfortable for everyone involved.

There was a lever somewhere inside him, a hidden switch: once flipped past a certain point, he found it impossible to stay externally present when approached or asked anything. He'd retreat into his head like a hermit crab scrambling for its shell, watching himself from the inside while his mouth moved on autopilot.

He'd been doing this self-observation thing for years—like a long-term psychoanalysis experiment he was conducting on himself. Spotting the patterns, cataloguing the behaviors, but somehow never quite managing to rewire the code when it mattered.

But Brayne had been teaching him how to tune into new frequencies. Michael's life was changing—not the external scaffolding of routines and responsibilities, but the inner landscape that nobody else could see. Same apartment, same grocery runs, same Netflix queue. Completely different vibration humming in his bones.

Yesterday, after Brayne had dropped that cryptic line about anchors calling to each other, they'd spent hours refining the practice. Learning how to find a frequency and hold it without losing it to anxiety or analysis. Michael had fallen asleep in that tuned state, exhausted but strangely buoyant.

When he woke the next morning, he felt clearer than he had in years. Steady. Grounded.

But the room was wrong.

Books on his desk rearranged in a different order. A chair angled a few degrees to the left. Papers stacked in a way he never stacked them—neat, purposeful, like someone had been organizing his thoughts while he slept.

Small shifts, but undeniable.

Anchors, the word flashed into his mind like a notification. Anchors don't wait to be found. They call each other.

\---

Michael rubbed his temples, staring at the crooked chair like it held the answer to a riddle he didn't remember asking. What did it mean to be an anchor? A stabilizer in rough seas? A weight pulling something down toward solid ground? Or maybe the opposite—a tether to keep from drifting into complete chaos.

He turned the word over like a worry stone. Maybe he was supposed to be anchoring reality itself. Or maybe anchors were people—those whose resonance matched his, keeping him from slipping fully into delusion.

That's when Brayne's voice dropped into his head, dry and amused as always.

You're overcomplicating it, Mikey. An anchor isn't a metaphor. It's a frequency-holder. Someone who doesn't collapse when the paradox wobbles. That's all. You'll know them because the field doesn't scatter around them—it harmonizes.

Michael exhaled, equal parts relieved and unnerved. The word felt smaller now, but heavier too. Not a mystical title or a savior complex. Just a function in the system.

He couldn't stay in the apartment. The walls felt like they were pulsing with afterimages of his mental diagrams. He needed air, movement, something ordinary to test his new perception against.

\---

He wandered to a café he'd passed a hundred times but never entered—one of those aggressively neutral places with exposed brick and Edison bulbs that looked like every other gentrified coffee shop in America.

The hum of conversation hit him at first, but underneath it, a deeper pulse emerged. A resonance so steady it felt like a low note vibrating through the floorboards, cutting through the ambient chaos of laptops clicking and espresso machines hissing.

He scanned the room, trying not to look like he was scanning. People hunched over screens, scrolling phones, conducting the usual rituals of caffeinated productivity. And then—at a corner table, someone who seemed... still.

Not frozen, but settled. Like gravity bent differently around them.

The stranger had sharp eyes that moved too fast, taking in everything—the barista's mood, the couple arguing quietly by the window, the way Michael was obviously trying not to stare. But beneath the hypervigilance was something else. A steadiness that didn't match the restless energy.

Michael swallowed, nerves tangling in his chest. "Uh... hey. This might sound weird, but... do you feel... different?"

The stranger raised an eyebrow, blinked twice like he was recalibrating. "Different how? Like gluten-free different, or like 'the wallpaper's humming equations at me' different?"

Michael's jaw dropped. "More the... humming equations."

A smirk cracked across the stranger's face. "Finally. Someone else admits they hear it. Thought I was going nuts." He tapped the table in a syncopated rhythm, like knocking on reality's architecture to see if it was still structurally sound. "Sit down before you combust."

Michael slid into the opposite chair, awkward and jittery, trying to catch the frequency Brayne had told him to notice. But what hit him first wasn't the steadiness—it was the way this person's sentences cut through the static in his head like someone rewiring him in real time.

"You look like the type who thinks he's the only one," the stranger said, half-challenging, half-inviting. "Newsflash: you're not special. You're just finally tuned in."

It landed like a punch and a lifeline at once.

\---

They talked for two hours. The stranger introduced himself as Jordan, but something in his energy felt more angular than that soft name suggested. He had opinions about everything—broken phone apps, the psychology of coffee shop design, why certain songs made reality feel more solid.

"It's all designed to fail," Jordan said, launching into a rant loud enough that heads turned. "Every glitch is just them pushing you toward the upgrade. Capitalism as a subscription service for your patience."

Michael half-laughed, half-winced—because it was funny, and because it was true. But the frequency beneath Jordan's words hit him harder: frustration that wasn't just about technology. Pain bleeding sideways through sarcasm.

Anchors hold, Mikey. But even anchors splinter if you don't patch the cracks.

Michael hesitated, palms sweating. Then he leaned into the humming practice Brayne had been teaching him—feeling Jordan's jagged resonance and offering steadiness back. Not words. Not advice. Just resonance—a breath carrying the message: I can hold this weight with you.

Something shifted. Jordan blinked mid-rant, like static had suddenly cut out of his internal radio.

"...What did you just do?"

Michael stammered, "Nothing. Just... breathing."

"Bullshit." Jordan leaned in, eyes sharp with something between suspicion and wonder. "You tuned me. Like a damn radio dial."

The accusation wasn't angry—more like astonished. Then a grin split across his face, and Michael caught a glimpse of who Jordan might be underneath all that crackling energy.

"If you can actually do that," Jordan said, "we need more of you. Of us. If there are more of us, we're not a glitch. We're the math."

The words vibrated through Michael like a solved equation, both terrifying and inevitable.

Michael's chest tightened with an emotion he couldn't name. Terror and excitement wrestling for control. The weight of responsibility and the relief of no longer being alone, all tangled up in his sternum.

Anchors converge, Mikey. This is just the beginning.

Later, Michael would call him Angstrom—the Unrelenting Equation. But for now, Jordan was enough. Someone else who heard the humming. Someone else who didn't collapse when reality got wobbly.

Someone else who was tired of being the only one.
