Episode 8

Michael's body felt like a drained battery, circuits still humming with phantom current from the seventy-two-hour download. He surfaced from sleep like a drowning man breaking water—thirty-six hours gone, maybe more. His body felt like it had been running emergency repairs, sealing his eyes and mouth with the crust of sickness. You know this feeling if you've ever been truly sick: that moment when your body announces it's been running damage control while you were away.

But today was different. Instead of the usual dread crawling up his spine, something else hummed in his chest. Fantasy. Impossibility. The electric anticipation that the world might actually bend if you push it right.

He pulled the scattered papers toward him—pages he and Gandi had covered in wild theories over the past few days. Reading them now, not in the fever-rush of creation but fragment by fragment, a different question surfaced: What if this isn't just theory?

You've done this before—convinced yourself you can meditate your way out of your problems. Michael spent the day cycling through every technique he'd half-remembered: breath counts that made him dizzy, mantras that turned to noise, visualizations collapsing the instant he tried to hold them.

The room remained stubbornly ordinary. His back ached from sitting. His mind felt scraped raw.

By nightfall, he'd given up. Slumped in his chair, no technique left untried, no diagram left untraced.

It happened when he wasn't trying.

His attention drifted while measuring insulin—the ritual so automatic now his hands could do it in his sleep. Phone screen glowing, numbers calculating, syringe filling. For the briefest instant, he wasn't managing his disease or correcting dosages. He was somewhere deeper, humming a wish through the marrow of his thought:

I wish I weren't diabetic.

Not a sentence. A frequency.

The shift started in his blood.

You know that feeling when your body suddenly remembers what "normal" felt like? That steadiness he hadn't experienced since childhood flooded through him. His skin tingled with the shock of equilibrium. Without test strips or monitors, he knew—the patch had taken.

Joy burned through him like electricity. He had done it.

It couldn't be real. But his blood said otherwise.

The healing held. His blood ran clean and steady, untouched by the jagged glucose spikes that had carved grooves in his life.

But everything has a price.

His memory split like a cracked mirror.

In one shard: the boy stabbing orange peels with training syringes, learning to live inside the cage of his disease.

In another: legs that could run forever, a body that had never known insulin, never counted carbs, never woken to the metallic taste of low blood sugar.

Both were true. Both demanded belief at the same time.

The paradox wasn't abstract—it was physical. Vision doubling, heart stuttering, lungs forgetting which timeline needed air. Two versions of himself fighting for the same present moment.

And in that overlap, reality itself began to stutter.

Michael clawed at the chair arms, desperate for something solid. He named objects in the room—lamp, desk, window—counted breaths, pressed his feet against the floor. The grounding techniques worked for seconds before the split in his memory yawned wider.

Then another voice entered the chaos. Low, sardonic, steady as bedrock.

"Grounding isn't a checklist, Michael." Brayne Snax's presence hummed through his skull. "It's resonance. Stop trying to pin yourself to one timeline. Anchor both. Feel the weight of them overlap."

The words didn't just calm him—they reoriented him completely.

His breath synced with the pulse in his palms. The floor beneath his feet seemed to recognize him, vibrating at the same frequency as his bones. The paradox didn't vanish, but the panic receded. He wasn't being torn apart; he was being stretched wide enough to hold both truths.

Here's what happened next: Reality found a way to reorganize itself.

Not in some earth-shattering, cosmic way. The world didn't notice. Your coffee still tastes the same. Traffic lights still change on schedule.

But for Michael? Everything changed.

With the paradox properly integrated, reality found a way to reorganize itself around him. Not dramatically—the world kept spinning, coffee kept brewing, traffic lights kept cycling. But something fundamental had shifted in the architecture of what was possible.

Michael trembled, two timelines humming through him like tuning forks finally struck into the same note.
