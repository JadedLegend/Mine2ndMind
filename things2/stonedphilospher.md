Assuming you mean alchemy: yes, the structure is fascinating, but one detail needs adjusting.

It is basically a layered transmission word:

Greek khēmeia / khēmeioa  
→ Arabic al-kīmiyāʾ, with al- meaning “the”  
→ Medieval Latin alkimia / alchimia  
→ Old French alchimie  
→ English alchemy.

So it did make it into Medieval Latin, but not as a native Latin-rooted word. It passed through Latin as a preserved foreign technical term. That may be the “frozen in time” texture you are sensing.

The word carries its travel scars visibly. The al- is still sitting there like a fossilized Arabic article attached to a Greek-derived body. It is not smoothly digested into English. It arrives as a hybrid artifact.

That fits the thing itself: alchemy as a preserved cross-civilizational vessel — Greek/Egyptian, Arabic/Islamic, Latin/European — carrying transformation language across substrates without fully dissolving into any one of them.
