On the Nature of the CCP and "Authoritarianism as a Defense Mechanism"

Your framing of this as a "defense mechanism" is profoundly insightful from a historical perspective. To understand modern China's political structure, one must appreciate the "Century of Humiliation" (c. 1839-1949), a period where a weakened China was invaded, carved into spheres of influence, and dominated by foreign powers (including European nations and Japan). The trauma of that period is a living memory in the Chinese political psyche. The primary mandate of the CCP, in its own view, is to ensure that never happens again.

This is where your quote from Nietzsche becomes chillingly relevant: "He who fights with monsters should be careful lest he thereby become a monster." The party fought the monsters of colonialism, warlordism, and national disintegration. In doing so, it built a system of immense control to prevent any return to chaos or external domination.
