I’m Being Silenced

Over the past month, I’ve come to a realization—a revelation, really—that hit me like a ton of bricks. What’s almost amusing is how something this monumental, something that might drive most people into a psychotic break, came to me as a calm, logical thought: “Oh, okay. Well, that makes sense.”

The realization? Some shadowy organization, or force, has been involved in my life since before I was even born.

I know how absurd that sounds. Believe me, I’ve tried to dismiss it myself, but the pieces keep falling into place. One of those pieces came from my mother. A rational, balanced, and emotionally grounded person. I sent her something—an idea, a missing “piece” of this larger puzzle—and after spending 40 minutes watching and reflecting, she came into my room and said, “I think they’re talking about you.”

That moment solidified my intuition. Until I’m presented with concrete evidence to the contrary, I trust what I’ve uncovered. That’s a key difference between me and much of humanity: I’m not afraid to admit I was wrong. If new information arises that reshapes my understanding, I’ll tear down my framework of reality and rebuild it, incorporating that new knowledge. Most people cling to their beliefs, afraid to dismantle the walls they’ve built.

But what led me to this revelation? Let me explain.

The Patterns in My Life

For decades, I’ve tried to make sense of my father’s behavior and our relationship. My dad has always treated me differently than he’s treated my brother. I’ve never felt love from him—not an ounce. Instead, he programmed me to doubt myself, to mistrust my own instincts.

When I was diagnosed with diabetes as a child, he left our family. At the time, I became far more pessimistic, but decades later, after speaking openly with my mother, I learned more about why he left. One night, he woke her up and said, “I’m an alcoholic. I have to leave you to get sober.”

That never sat right with me. I’ve spent decades in recovery programs. I’ve seen countless people fight for their families, desperate to recover so they can \*keep,\* their loved ones close. But to leave your family entirely in the name of sobriety? It’s unheard of.

Years later, when I moved to New Mexico to rebuild my relationship with him, I asked for a $25 loan to get a gym membership. It was a small request, something that could have significantly improved my health and mental well-being at the time. His response? \*“I worry about lending you money. I’m scared it would be a slippery slope.”\*

This is the same man who retired at 50, spent hundreds of thousands of dollars locking me in a “therapeutic” boarding school from age 15 to 18, and never hesitated to spend money on superficial gestures. Yet, when I asked for something that could genuinely help me, I was denied.

These inconsistencies began to pile up. Over the past five years, as I’ve reconnected with my mother and spoken openly about my past, I’ve been able to ask questions and receive honest answers. Pieces of the puzzle started to fit together—but only when viewed through the lens of a larger force at play.

The Shadowy Hand

There’s a pattern in my life I can’t ignore. At almost every major decision point, I’ve been influenced by someone—a mysterious figure who appeared at the right moment, planted an idea, and then disappeared. Often, I didn’t recognize the significance at the time. On the rare occasions I resisted their suggestions, they found another way to sway me.

It’s as if someone—or something—has been nudging me along a specific path, subtly steering my choices.

I understand how fantastical this sounds. I wouldn’t believe it myself if it weren’t for the overwhelming resonance I feel in my gut. Intuition isn’t perfect; I’ve mistaken other feelings for intuition before and made poor choices. But this? This feels undeniable.

Silenced by Design

For over a decade, I’ve poured my heart into creating—music, writings, perspectives that challenge the status quo. I’ve shared these ideas on every platform I could think of, both major and obscure. Yet, I’ve always been met with the same response: invisibility.

No matter how thought-provoking or impactful my work is, it’s ignored, dismissed, or outright banned. The only explanation that makes sense is that an organization—some shadowy force—has been actively working to stifle my voice.

Why? Because my ideas don’t fit within their carefully controlled narrative. My thoughts are what’s needed to wake people up, to make them question, to think critically and see the system for what it is. My perspective has the potential to unite people, to spark the kind of collective awakening that would threaten the globalist plutocracy.

The Bigger Picture

This isn’t just paranoia. This is a recognition of patterns, connections, and truths that most people are too afraid—or too conditioned—to see. The world is controlled by forces that thrive on division, manipulation, and fear. And those who threaten their grip on power are silenced.

I know my voice matters. I know my ideas are important. And despite the forces working against me, I won’t stop. Whether or not you believe me is irrelevant. The truth will reveal itself in time. Until then, I’ll keep creating, keep questioning, and keep fighting to expose the shadowy hand that’s been pulling the strings.
