Damn, I wish that had been on video. It would’ve gone viral—almost guaranteed.

It happened at the Papa John's on Clark, near Beneva. By far the rudest store, collectively, that I’ve ever encountered in all my time living in a consumerist, capitalist society.

So, I walk in and ask about my order. They tell me, rudely, "Five minutes." Like, how do you even manage to throw off a rude vibe with a question as simple as that? Is this place sitting on a bad-vibe vortex or something?

Anyway, I come back after five minutes. This time, they tell me they messed up my order and I’d need to wait another seven minutes. Annoying, but whatever—I waited. Here's where it gets interesting.

Seven minutes later, I stick my head in to check. I ask, “Is it ready?” Some guy who must’ve just clocked in screams at me, “Look at the goddamn door! Go get your bag!”

Apparently, there’s a tiny sticker on the door that says DoorDash drivers are supposed to bring their bags in. Not once in my previous visits did anyone mention this. Most places are fine with me tossing the pizza in the bag once I’m back at the car.

Still, I stayed respectful and followed instructions. I went back to my car, got the red bag, and returned. Then I asked again, "Okay, where's my pizza?"

The same guy goes, "It's gonna be another five minutes."

At that point, I was done. I said, “What? I was told seven minutes, seven minutes ago. And before that, I was told five minutes. What’s going on?” Then I let loose. I told the entire store exactly what I thought of them—that they are, without question, the rudest store I have ever been in.

That’s when things escalated. The guy starts yelling again, trying to intimidate me into backing down. I wasn’t having it. I cut him off mid-rant, yelling louder than him: "FUCK YOU. YOU ARE A PIECE OF SHIT. YOU ARE A TERRIBLE HUMAN BEING. FUCK YOU."

At that point, he starts pacing, screaming, and gesturing wildly. He clocked out and got right up in my face, trying to bait me into stepping outside to fight. This happened three or four times in total.

“You don’t wanna see me, bruh,” he kept yelling. “You’re a pussy. Let’s step outside and handle this.”

I just laughed and said, “Handle what? You suck. Fact. Done.”

All the while, his manager stood there shaking, absolutely terrified. A 50-something-year-old white guy, frozen. The new guy tossing dough in the corner had a "WTF is happening" look on his face. The crusty veteran pulling pizzas out of the oven was trying her hardest not to burst out laughing.

I just kept repeating, “Fuck you. You suck. Hit me and enjoy jail, you piece of human garbage. You’re a sorry excuse for a person.”

It’s been a long time since I’ve yelled “FUCK YOU” directly into someone’s face. Inches away, looking them dead in the eye. Honestly, I don’t know if I’ve ever done that before. But it was exhilarating.

This guy got so close, nose to nose, and barked, “What’d you say, bro?”

I yelled it again. “FUCK YOU.”

Looking back, it was oddly fun. I was pushing him as far as I could, legally.

Don’t get me wrong—I'm all for fighting to defend ideals or in self-defense. I’ve been in a few fights myself, won some, lost some. But I’ve never understood people who use fighting to solve everything. It’s juvenile.

This whole scene reminded me of my late friend Aaron. He used to settle disagreements with his fists—until he learned there were better ways. But that mindset, that reflex, gets ingrained in you, especially when you grow up institutionalized.

Aaron spent years in juvie, gladiator camps, and big-boy prison. By the time he hit 40, he’d done at least 15, maybe 20 years behind bars. All that time isolated, institutionalized, and criminalized—it shapes you.

Prison isn’t about rehabilitation. It’s a breeding ground for bad habits and worse ideas. You take a decent person, put them in with bad people, and guess what? They pick up bad traits. It’s not rocket science.

It’s the same with so many of these “rehabilitation” systems—boarding schools, detox centers, halfway houses. They’re designed to help, but they end up reinforcing the problems they claim to fix.

But this guy? Fighting over being called out for being a shitty employee at a shitty pizza shop? That’s just hilarious to me. If calling them out makes me a bad guy, fine—I’ll own it.

Look, I get that people have their own struggles. I respect that. What I don’t respect is people being disrespectful to others for no reason. Respect is a two-way street—you give it to get it.

For so long, I let people like this walk all over me. And that adds to the story I keep playing in my head, over and over. But not anymore. I’m done being walked on.
