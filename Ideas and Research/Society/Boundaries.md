I've been getting better at setting boundaries, but I still feel really bad when I have to enforce them. Like, really bad. For example, I held myself back and helped my friend for six months. I put so much energy into trying to help her turn her life around. I eventually got her housing in West Palm and tried to set her up for success. Things started to go wrong. I gave her someone to connect to—someone who could help. She didn’t make the effort. Things just kept unraveling. And now, I still feel guilty, even though I know I did everything I could.

It’s crazy to me how much I care about people, but people don’t seem to care for me in the same way.

The thing about a loaded word like \*care\* is that it can mean so many different things to so many people. When I say I care, I don’t mean simply providing for someone or looking after their needs. I mean \*serious attention and consideration\*, an effort to truly understand someone, to help without causing more harm.

And because I care so deeply, I become emotionally invested—too emotionally invested. I throw myself into the relationship or the situation, and it turns me into a walking target for anyone willing to sink their fangs into me.

It would be nice, just once, to find someone genuine. I know I’m not at a place in my life right now where I can have a healthy romantic relationship. But I still want it. I want someone I can settle down with. That was my focus in my last relationship, but it quickly went sideways.

When things went bad, I ended it. Even after breaking up, I helped her for six months. I thought I was doing the right thing, but it left me drained.

Since she’s been gone, it’s been lonely. But honestly? Having just one person isn’t all that different from being alone. And god, there’s nothing I hate more than inconsequential fights over stupid bullshit.

After the romantic love was gone, there was still love there—it had just changed. But I knew it wasn’t sustainable anymore.

I’ll never forget the first time she asked me about an ex commenting on one of my Facebook posts or something like that. She was upset, and we had a discussion about it. I told her the truth: “Yes, I’m still in love with pretty much all of my exes.” She couldn’t understand that, and I couldn’t explain it in a way that made sense to her.

For me, that love doesn’t mean I want to be with them. It’s not romantic anymore. It’s just that the connection and care don’t disappear entirely when the relationship ends. I guess that’s another reason why I get so emotionally drained—I don’t know how to let go of the love I’ve felt for people, even after things change or fall apart.

Now, here I am, talking to her again, and it’s stressing me out. I feel that old familiar heaviness creeping back in. Time to go take a Klonopin and try to calm myself down.
