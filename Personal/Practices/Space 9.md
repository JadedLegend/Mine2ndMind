Relational Alignment to Actuality

Once 6 has been somatically experienced.

9 becomes available.

3 was you, and the forms you wear.

6 was you, and the others you sail towards.

9 is you, the field, and what is actually (t)here.

Not the story about here.

Here.

Actuality is the part that does not require belief to remain true.

It’s been here since well before the identity arrived.

It’s always been right here. Underneath all relational aspects of existence.

It will remain after the costume is set down.

The word "align" hides a smaller word inside it.

A line.

To align is to come onto the same line as something.

Not above it. Not against it. Alongside.

We spend a remarkable amount of effort attempting to align to a specific overlay.

  
The story of who we are.

The story of who they are.

  
  
The story of what this moment is supposed to be.

The story of what we wished this moment was.

  
The overlay is not a lie.

The overlay is not the truth.  

Our most prominent method used in attempt to retain continuity, can be gestured towards as a cognitive illusory scaffolding, of sorts.

  
We’re often left to try and map forces we don’t fully understand.

Useful. Rarely accurate. Almost never current.

Actuality is the territory the map keeps trying to describe.

The territory does not argue with the map.

It simply remains the territory.

Misalignment tends to announce itself.

A friction. A flinch. A sentence that sounds true and feels false.

The body often registers the gap before the mind agrees there is one.

This may be worth trusting more than it usually is.

Alignment to actuality is not agreement with actuality.

You can meet what is here and still grieve it.

You can align with a fact and still work to change it.

Acceptance of the territory is not surrender of the journey.

What was 3's undercurrent.

What 6 called the water beneath the ships.

  
Is simply this.

What is actually happening.

Underneath what we are telling ourselves about it.

  
Most suffering seems to live in the gap.

The distance between the moment as it is, and the moment as we insist it should have been.

Close the gap and the suffering has less to stand on.

  
The gap does not always close by changing the world.

The gap often begins closing through release, detachment, or surrender.

  
A question, maybe worth holding:

What is actually here — right now — before I name it?

  
And the harder one beneath it:

Am I willing to be here, even if here is not where I wanted to be?

Alignment is not an elusive end state.

It’s a line where commitment can land, and return. Relationally.

Once you find yourself within the current, you will drift.

The overlay will redraw itself. The costume will creep back on.

This is not failure. This is the spiral.

  
First — notice what appears to be a gap.

Next — notice the gap noticing you.

Finally — step back onto the line.

  
The same recursion. Spiraling inward, outward, and through.

Aligned relation clears the translation between story and field.
