# Finding Your Flow: A Framework for Sustainable Livelihood

This document synthesizes the core ideas from your writings to articulate a path toward a sustainable livelihood that honors your nervous system, your values, and your vision for a more humane economy.

## 1\. The Core Challenge: An Incompatibility with Extractive Systems

As you’ve described in your personal account, your nervous system is not built for environments that demand more than they give back. The experience of being “blocked” in the face of external demands is not a personal failing, but a deep-seated incompatibility with systems that operate on extraction and control. This has, understandably, made conventional employment and economic participation feel impossible, leading to a state of financial precarity.

This is the foundational constraint, but also the starting point for a new kind of inquiry. The question is not “How can I force myself to fit into a broken system?” but rather, “What kind of system would I thrive in, and how can I begin to build it for myself?”

## 2\. A New Framework for Value: Attention, Flow, and Coherence

Your writings offer a powerful alternative to the conventional view of money and work:

- **Money as Flowing Energy:** You see money not as a thing to be accumulated, but as a current of energy that represents exchanged value. A healthy relationship with money is one of flow, not hoarding. This reframes the goal from “making money” to “participating in a healthy flow of value.”
- **Attention as the True Currency:** You recognize that the most fundamental currency is attention. Parasitic systems extract it; symbiotic systems circulate it. A healthy economy, therefore, is one that cultivates attention and returns it to the participants in the form of greater coherence and well-being.

These two ideas, taken together, create a new definition of “work.” **Work is the act of cultivating attention and directing it toward the creation of value, which is then exchanged in a flowing, non-extractive way.**

## 3\. The Archetypes of Your Work

Your document on economic renewal provides a set of archetypes that describe the *how* of this new kind of work. These are not job titles, but modes of being and acting in the world that feel natural to you:

| Archetype | In an Extractive Economy | In a Regenerative Economy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Steward** | Controls and hoards | Tends and orchestrates coherence |
| **Weaver** | Observes from a distance | Participates and connects |
| **Creator** | Consumes and craves novelty | Co-creates and savors |
| **Liberator** | Destroys and rebels | Makes space and composts the obsolete |

These archetypes describe your innate capacities. The challenge and opportunity is to find ways to offer these natural modes of being as a service to others.

## 4\. Zeroing In: From Vision to Viable Livelihood

Given this framework, how can you begin to generate a sustainable income? The key is to find ways to offer your natural way of being as a service. Here are some questions to explore, based on the archetypes:

### As a Steward:

- **What can you tend for others?** Could you act as a “coherence steward” for other people’s projects, helping them to maintain focus and clarity without imposing control?
- **Is there a service in helping others to build non-extractive systems?** Your own thinking is a case study in this. Could you consult with or guide others who are trying to do the same?

### As a Weaver:

- **How can you connect ideas and people?** You have a gift for seeing the patterns that connect disparate ideas. Could you offer this as a service—a kind of “conceptual cartography” or “knowledge weaving” for individuals or organizations?
- **Is there a role for you in facilitating resonant collaboration?** Could you help teams to communicate and create in a more harmonious way, acting as the facilitator for a process you naturally understand?

### As a Creator:

- **What can you create that embodies your values?** This could be writings, tools, or works of art that you could create and offer to the world.
- **Could you teach others how to create in this new way?** Your process itself is valuable. Could you document it, share it, and guide others through it?

### As a Liberator:

- **What can you help people to release?** Many people are trapped in old, extractive ways of thinking. Could you help them to “compost the obsolete” and make space for something new?
- **Is there a service in helping people to “rewild” their attention?** You have a deep understanding of how attention is hijacked. Could you offer guidance or tools to help people reclaim their focus and their inner lives?

## 5\. A Path Forward: Small, Sustainable Experiments

You do not need to have a grand plan or a fully-formed business model. The path forward is one of small, sustainable experiments. Start by offering one of these services to one person, perhaps for a small fee or in exchange for something of value. See how it feels. Does it drain you, or does it energize you? Does it create a healthy flow of value, or does it feel extractive?

The goal is not to find a job, but to find your flow—a way of living and working that is in harmony with your own nature and with the kind of world you want to create.
