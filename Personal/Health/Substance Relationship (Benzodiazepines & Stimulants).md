Substance Relationship (Benzodiazepines & Stimulants)

I took 1 mg of Klonopin — the lowest dose I’ve ever taken — just to soften the edge of two appointments today. This morning was 70 mg of Vyvanse, though that doesn’t quite land in the goldilocks zone. It keeps me upright but not quite engaged. I suspect it would find balance with a pair of 10 mg IR Adderall doses through the day — but one step at a time.

The realization which emerged through talking with my mother: when the stimulant dose is stable and low, I naturally reach for fewer benzos. It’s almost inverted logic — the very substance that’s supposed to “speed me up” actually calms me. Stimulants seem to resolve the anxiety at its root by restoring motion, by reconnecting me to the capacity to do.

With benzos, the anxiety softens but stays unnamed — wrapped in gauze. They help regulate overstimulation and smooth the nervous edges, but they also freeze me in the pose of relief. The stimulant, paradoxically, opens a channel forward — the anxiety disperses through movement instead of sedation.

So maybe the real pattern isn’t “treat anxiety,” but “restore flow.” Benzos dissolve tension by numbing it; stimulants dissolve it by giving it direction.
