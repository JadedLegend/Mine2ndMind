The Cult of Pop Culture

It’s remarkable to me how so many people look up to the ignorant, small-brained individuals who dominate "popular" culture. The majority of what exists in pop culture is so transparently shallow, designed to entertain, distract, and manipulate.

Shame on you if you can’t see through it. The entertainment you consume is often created with one purpose: to divide, reinforce negative beliefs, and keep you pacified with dopamine hits. It’s all a game, played at the expense of critical thinking and true awareness.

The Subconscious Mind: A Hidden Power

The subconscious mind is a mystery to most people. If you’re not aware of its influence, you’re at the mercy of those who exploit it. I highly recommend reading Carl Jung, Daniel Dennett, Joseph Murphy, Maxwell Maltz, and Joe Dispenza.

Understanding the subconscious changed the way I view everything—especially music. Pop music, for example, is crafted with specific intentions: to keep you dumb, happy, and in line.

But real happiness doesn’t come from ignorance. It comes from facing the shit you don’t want to deal with. Once you do, you’ll understand what true fulfillment feels like.

The Purpose of My Art

Everything I write and create is meant to challenge resistance and open minds. My music isn’t just about entertainment; it’s about breaking barriers, exposing truths, and offering a glimpse of something deeper.

The process feels channeled at times. There’s a stark difference between songs that pour out effortlessly and those I force myself to work on. The former feels like something beyond me, while the latter lacks that same spark.

I know the intention behind my art: to help people see the world differently and confront the ineffable truths that can’t be captured in words alone.

The Struggle with Motivation

But I worry. I worry that much of my motivation comes from the hope of recognition—or more importantly, from a genuine desire to help others. Without that, would I still create? Would the drive remain if I made everything private?

It’s a terrifying thought, the idea that without external connection, I might lose the will to write, to create, to share.

But then again, maybe none of it matters. Maybe it’s all inconsequential in the grand scheme of things.
