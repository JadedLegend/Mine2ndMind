When I turned 18, my mom had to bring the police to get me out of that hellhole. Imagine that—your own parent needing law enforcement to free you, because my dad, Chuck, and the staff at the so-called therapeutic boarding school I was sent to wanted to keep me there \*against my will.\* Even though I was legally an adult.

Looking back, it fills me with rage. A counselor named Roma, a man who routinely pinned down 13-year-old kids, was part of this twisted system. At this “school,” we were forced to sit in group therapy sessions for hours—sometimes from dawn until well past dusk—for weeks on end. The methods they used were beyond cruel. If I told you everything, you'd think I was lying. But I’m not.

These were \*adults\*—educated enough to know better, right? Where were the scientific studies to back their “methods”? This wasn’t cognitive therapy. This was punishment. Isolation. Humiliation. They deprived and punished certain kids while rewarding others for the smallest things. It was blatant favoritism designed to divide us and break us down.

It was miserable. My family had its problems, but at least I had my mom. My dad? He was a mess, but nothing prepared me for this new, ultra-dysfunctional "family." The place was run by broken people who treated us like inmates, not children. And somehow, my dad kept telling me “this was helping.”

I remember witnessing my fair share of "holds." A hold was a violent restraint used on a child to force them into compliance. The Netflix series \*The Program\* talks about how schools like this one regularly filed hundreds of hold forms in a single month. I remember one kid, being held down five times in a single month because he hated living there and dared to act out. I was held a few times myself. Let me tell you—those holds weren’t discipline. They were abusive control mechanisms.

The school changed its name three times while I was there. Suspicious, right? Probably to dodge lawsuits. And there were “lawsuits.” They had to rebrand because they kept breaking laws involving minors. It was a shitty place, run by shitty people. We were bad kids, sure, but instead of healing, they made us worse. Much worse.

They forced us into these marathon group therapy sessions, sometimes 12+ hours, to sit in circles and parrot back the stories they \*told\* us about ourselves. They told us what our problems were, who we were, and what we had to do to fix ourselves. There was no self-discovery. It was \*brainwashing.\* Plain and simple.

And the man behind it all? Chuck Kanner. A gambling addict, who, as far as I could tell, was barely making sense half the time. I’ve run into him six times since I left. Every time, he’d give me the same BS line: “Oh, we’re starting another school. You’d make a great counselor.” Are you kidding me? You’re a stain on my soul, and you think I’d ever go back to help you continue this cycle of abuse? Fuck you.

The trauma from that school still haunts me. They locked me in a library, by myself, 12+ hours a day, for three months when I was 15. I acted out so badly that they \*invented a Level 0\* just for me. And yet, I wasn’t the only one broken by this place. Most of my friends from that school are dead now—gone. The rest of us? Drug addiction, mental health issues, prison. That’s what this school prepared us for. That’s what this school created us to be.

I was in jail within two years of leaving. Eleven months and 29 days in Port Manatee. And you know what? Jail was easier to endure than that school. At least in jail, I wasn’t being gaslit into thinking the abuse was \*helping\* me.

The staff were complicit in all of it. They watched us shut down, lose our spirits, and fall apart, and they did nothing. Nothing but bark orders. The only two people there who seemed to genuinely care were David Forestier and Josh Harrold. I’ll never forget that. But the rest of them? They’re evil, child soul-snatching pieces of shit who will answer for their actions. Just as I’ve had to answer for mine.

This school didn’t rehabilitate anyone. It criminalized us. It turned us into addicts, dealers, and broken adults. We missed our families. We missed being kids. Instead, we were trapped in a place that brainwashed us into believing \*their\* version of our lives. We were punished until we conformed, and if we didn’t? They made our lives hell.

It wasn’t just this school. The tactics they used are everywhere—in cults, in abusive programs, in Scientology. I know because I’ve seen it. I went to a Narconon rehab, another nightmare. They made us do training routines eerily similar to the brainwashing at school. Sitting in silence, staring for hours, enduring verbal abuse. It’s all the same playbook.

This system doesn’t rehabilitate; it profits off of pain. They gamed state funds and parents’ wallets, all while providing nothing—no GEDs, no real education, nothing. They destroyed lives and made millions doing it.

If you’ve seen “The Program” on Netflix, that was my life from 15 to 18. It’s the same story: a shady, abusive system that funneled money to one guy and his family. Millions and millions. Meanwhile, kids like me were left with scars we’ll carry for the rest of our lives.

Fuck that school. Fuck the people who ran it. And fuck the system that let it happen.
