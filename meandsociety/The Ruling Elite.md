The Ruling Elite. The Systems in Place. How to Escape the Grip.

It’s frustrating to feel unheard, but the truth is, the systems in place are designed to keep us distracted, divided, and too overwhelmed to take action. That’s the brilliance of the ruling elite’s control—it’s subtle enough to seem normal and pervasive enough to feel insurmountable. But we must confront these systems and offer clear ways out.

The Ruling Elite: Fact, Not Theory

Let’s start with the facts: wealth inequality has reached astronomical levels. In a world of nearly 8 billion people, about 30,000 are centi-millionaires. That’s 0.000375% of the population controlling resources most of us can’t even imagine. Of these, an even smaller number—likely 3,000 to 5,000 individuals—constitutes the true ruling elite. These people aren’t on social media, they don’t tweet, and they don’t show up in tabloids. They operate behind the scenes, pulling the strings of corporations, governments, and even the media narratives we consume.

Publicly visible billionaires like Elon Musk, Bill Gates, and Jeff Bezos are distractions. They serve as lightning rods for public scrutiny, giving us figures to love, hate, or emulate, while the true ruling class remains hidden. Corporations like BlackRock and Vanguard, which together control over $20 trillion in global assets, are merely tools for these elite families. Their wealth isn’t just a number—it’s influence. They shape economies, dictate policies, and steer the cultural conversation, often without us realizing.

Capitalism: The Evolution of Slavery

Capitalism, for all its promises of opportunity and innovation, is the natural evolution of slavery. It rewards self-interest and exploitation, valuing profit over people. The system ensures that the vast majority of people are stuck in a cycle of poverty or financial instability, while a tiny fraction hoards unimaginable wealth.

Consider this: 90% of Americans earn less than $32,000 a year, while the cost of living has skyrocketed. Meanwhile, the ultra-wealthy use offshore accounts, tax loopholes, and financial instruments to grow their fortunes exponentially. It’s not hard work or merit that defines success in capitalism—it’s access to capital, connections, and often sheer luck.

The illusion of a “free market” is just that: an illusion. What we live in is a globalist plutocracy—a system where wealth determines power, and power perpetuates wealth. Over time, this plutocracy has evolved into a technocratic regime, where algorithms, data, and surveillance consolidate control even further. This is why issues like Jeffrey Epstein’s network vanish into obscurity; the justice system itself serves the elite.

How to Escape the Grip

Escaping this grip requires collective awakening and action. Here are some key points for consideration:

1\. Economic Literacy: Most people don’t fully understand how the system exploits them. By educating ourselves and others on wealth inequality, wage stagnation, and corporate influence, we can begin to dismantle the illusion of the “American Dream.”

2\. Refusing Division: The elite thrive on division—racial, political, and economic. Recognizing our shared struggles is the first step toward unity. The working class, regardless of background, has far more in common than the elite would have us believe.

3\. Rejecting the System: Universal Basic Income (UBI) has been floated as a solution, but it’s only a Band-Aid. The root issue is the system itself, which thrives on inequality. Imagine a world where basic needs like housing, healthcare, and education are rights, not privileges. We have the resources to make this happen, but it requires a fundamental shift in values.

4\. Building Alternatives: Communities can create local economies, cooperatives, and systems of mutual aid that bypass the exploitative mechanisms of capitalism. From bartering systems to community land trusts, these alternatives weaken the elite’s control.

5\. Political Engagement: While many systems of governance are compromised, change is still possible through grassroots movements. Support candidates and policies that prioritize wealth redistribution, corporate accountability, and environmental sustainability.

6\. Technological Resistance: The rise of technocracy means our data and behavior are being weaponized against us. Decentralized technologies, privacy tools, and digital literacy can help individuals reclaim some autonomy.

7\. Conscious Consumerism: Every dollar spent is a vote for the kind of world we want to live in. Supporting small businesses, ethical brands, and sustainable practices can erode the market share of megacorporations.

Painting the Picture

Think of it this way: a few thousand people control the fate of 8 billion. They don’t need to use brute force; we agree to their rules every day by participating in their systems. We work for their companies, buy their products, and internalize their narratives. But what if we collectively said no? What if we redirected our energy, time, and resources toward systems that prioritize humanity over profit?

The ruling elite may seem untouchable, but their power is fragile. It depends on our complicity and ignorance. By refusing to play their game, we can begin to build a world that values life, community, and true freedom over greed and control.

This isn’t just idealism—it’s a roadmap. Every revolution in history started with a shift in consciousness. The question is: will we take the first step?
