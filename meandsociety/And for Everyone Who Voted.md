I hold no judgments. But I struggle to understand how we, as a collective species, continue to fail in seeing things as they likely are.

Our political system—a facade—still stands, despite its glaring failures. It astounds me. This is no democracy; it’s a technocratic plutocracy. For those who believe Trump—or anyone—will “fix everything,” I ask: how can you trust a man who mastered this game of capitalism, a system built on exploitation, division, and greed?

Capitalism was marketed as freedom and equality for a fleeting few decades, but its foundation is clear: it thrives on taking advantage of others, dividing humanity into haves and have-nots. Trump, long before becoming king of America, was already king of the haves. Winning at capitalism means prioritizing yourself above all else—at the expense of others. How can that be trusted?

We live in a world divided by imaginary lines, drawn by leaders who have historically murdered and stolen in the name of power. Yet, we pledge allegiance to these false boundaries and fractured nations. Instead of uniting, we cling to differences. The wars we once fought with bullets have shifted to battles of fear—fear of each other, fear of technological advances.

And America? A country founded to escape British tyranny, only to replicate that same tyranny. We stole land from Native Americans, erased their sacred knowledge, and justified the slaughter with arrogance. Imagine how different this land could have been if, instead of destruction, we’d chosen collaboration, respect, and coexistence. Instead, we repeated the same mistakes.

History doesn’t just rhyme; it’s on an endless loop. We’re told “history repeats,” but hearing it is far different from seeing it. It takes contemplation to truly witness the cycles, to feel them subjectively. When we do, the patterns become undeniable.
