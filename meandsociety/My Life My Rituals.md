In my life, there's an unshakable ritual: my six pre-rolled cones must always be ready. Thanks to the convenience of the RAW six-cone contraption, this simple habit has transformed into an effortless delight. But when I reach for a pre-roll and find none prepared, a wave of bittersweet emotion washes over me—sadness intertwined with a quiet excitement. These moments are more than the act of rolling; they represent a deeper connection to my essence—a fleeting ten-minute escape that makes me feel alive, grounded, and tethered to the earth beneath my feet.

People often tell me to take a tolerance break, to cut back. But I’ve grown weary of others dictating my path. After more than 30 years of following someone else’s script, I’ve discovered one profound truth: think for yourself. Embrace openness, yes—but never at the cost of losing your sense of self. Perhaps this revelation was a conversation with my own spirit, a reminder that the rest of the world can take a back seat to my own discoveries.

So few seem willing to entertain the notion that understanding a concept is vastly different from experiencing it firsthand. Words are mere symbols, fragile attempts to capture the vast internal landscapes we navigate daily. Sometimes, I write them solely for myself—a way to decode emotions that often elude articulation.

There’s an ancient Chinese saying I once stumbled upon in an Alan Watts book: \*“The finger points at the moon.”\* In our obsession with the finger, we often miss the brilliance of the moon entirely. How beautifully these proverbs and platitudes illuminate our journeys through time! They serve as guiding lights for those brave enough to carry the torch through tumultuous histories.

Yet, no matter how much we reflect, we can never truly grasp the past. It leaves behind remnants—trauma, structures, echoes—but the direct experience is forever lost.

Consider this: everything is happening simultaneously across a multitude of universes, stacked in a cosmic arrangement both bewildering and awe-inspiring. Our latest scientific revelations hint at this intricate tapestry, suggesting that time itself is merely a human construct—a tool we devised to navigate the cycles of life and nature’s rhythms.

Biologically, our bodies run on a clock far more profound than any alarm we set. I often envision my mind as an antenna, attuned to certain frequencies while the universe unfolds in paradoxes beyond comprehension. Quantum mechanics reveals this dance, showing us that all potentialities—past, present, and future—exist concurrently within an infinite web.

This is why so many spiritual practices emphasize living in the present moment. I’ve long felt the weight of time pressing upon me, though I can’t pinpoint how long it’s been. I recall a profound Ayahuasca experience where I was drawn into eternity, shown infinity. And yet, I’ve never been shown anything tangible—only felt things deeply.

That’s the paradox of using psychedelics for healing: the experiences are often ineffable, filled with beauty and mystery. Seasoned travelers remind me to trust the power of the substance, yet the day after every profound journey, everyone seems eager to share their insights, often echoing one another. I tend to remain quiet, still digesting revelations that unfurl slowly within me.

I often wish these experiences could be woven into a cohesive narrative, but I write trip reports only for myself. What I’ve noticed is striking: the way I articulate my journeys often feels as though I’ve taken an entirely different substance than those around me.

Perhaps it’s because I come from a realm beyond this one. My stepmother once told me I was the Archangel Michael, channeling multidimensional entities for over two hours as her voice shifted into distinct frequencies.

In the midst of this chaotic beauty, I hold onto the hope of manifesting my vision into the world. And yet, I carry a profound understanding: in the grand scheme of existence, nothing truly matters.

And maybe, just maybe, that’s the most liberating realization of all.
