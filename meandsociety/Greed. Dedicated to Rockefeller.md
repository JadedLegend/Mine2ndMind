It’s staggering to think about the sheer power and influence that figure represents. And yet, what amazes me more is how 8 billion people continue to allow the existence of so many unnecessary systems. Systems that promise one thing but, without fail, seem to deliver the exact opposite.

Take modern policing, for instance. Its origins trace back to the \*Slave Patrols\*. I won’t go into detail here—I know I’ll lose people if I do. But it’s important to point out where things started to understand why we’re where we are now. Systems built on greed, control, and manipulation tend to stay that way unless actively restructured. The foundation was never about serving the people; it was always about controlling them.

I doubt anyone on my friends list has ever held a position of \*true\* power. But if they did, I’d ask: Are these systems really serving humanity, or are they just maintaining a status quo designed to benefit the few?

Let’s talk about modern medicine.

Do you know how it began in the late 1800s and early 1900s? John D. Rockefeller—yes, the oil tycoon—realized that the new pills being manufactured relied on petroleum. And since he essentially controlled the oil market, he saw an opportunity to monopolize medicine too.

Rockefeller teamed up with fellow tycoon Andrew Carnegie, and together they reshaped the entire medical industry. They lobbied Congress (as the largest political donors, they didn’t need to work too hard) and got a series of licensing laws passed. These laws effectively shut down holistic colleges and outlawed natural medicine. What remained was a system entirely dependent on pharmaceuticals—many of which relied on Rockefeller’s oil.

But it didn’t stop there. They established "re-education centers" to retrain medical practitioners in this new, profit-driven model of medicine. Sound familiar? It’s eerily similar to re-education tactics we’ve seen in authoritarian regimes.

And here we are today, with a healthcare system built on the idea that \*sick people make better customers\*. The focus was never truly on healing—it was on profit.
