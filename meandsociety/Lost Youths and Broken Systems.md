Lost Youths and Broken Systems

Life often feels like a web of contradictions, especially when you're young and labeled before you've even had the chance to understand yourself.

I was one of the countless lost youths—thrown into programs meant to "fix" me but only reinforced the sickness I was told I had. From the age of 15, I was diagnosed as an addict, my identity reduced to a single word: “addiction”.

No one stopped to ask the right questions. No one cared to listen. My dad, who abandoned my family, spent hundreds of thousands of dollars to send me away, but we never had a real connection. The truth is, I didn’t need those programs. I needed to be heard.

Instead, I was surrounded by other miserable, depressed teenagers, forced to attend five meetings a week. At every step, they told me what to think, what to feel, what to do—pushing me further from myself.

What I really needed was someone who saw me for who I was. Someone to nurture my positive thoughts, help me navigate the negative, and teach me that balance is essential. Instead, I was fed a narrative that my "disease" defined me, shaping a reality I’m still paying the price for.

The Machinery of Control

These systems—12 steps, therapeutic boarding schools, wilderness programs, juvenile detention centers—are designed not to heal but to create recurring customers. Society isn't structured to help us grow; it's built to keep us dependent.

Even our education system reinforces this. You can graduate from the most prestigious university, yet if you lack the ability to think critically for yourself, you're still trapped. The goal seems to be keeping us reliant on technology and external validation, so we never question anything.

It’s like the world is a giant machine, built to control, consume, and discard us. A handful of people—those with power and resources—pull the strings, hiding behind masks while they profit from our pain.

Reclaiming Myself

Looking back, I see the "could haves" and "should haves." If only someone had truly listened to me. If only I had been allowed to find myself instead of being pushed into someone else’s mold.

But I’ve learned to laugh at the absurdity of it all. Life hasn’t broken me completely, though it’s tried. I’ve found a rhythm—a passion within my writing, my music, my thoughts.

Even when I feel like I’m on the outside, a “creepy freak” people seldom speak to, I keep going. Because I know this: the systems are flawed, the establishment is broken, and we need to start over.

Not just tweak the existing structure, but rebuild from the ground up. From the beginning. That’s the only way forward.
