Every type of primate is known for intergroup violence—except bonobos. Oh, now I get your name, Bonobo.

Interestingly, bonobos are not only known for their peaceful nature but also for their complex and, let’s just say, \*very liberal\* sexual relationships. Their societies are unique in ways that both intrigue and challenge our understanding of social behavior.

This article I came across dives into theories and studies on intergroup cohesion and out-group aggression among primates. It’s one of those hyperfocused, dense pieces—much like my writing—that not everyone has the patience for.

Here’s the gist: most primates have the ability to work together, but when confronted with outsiders, they often resort to violence. Bonobos, however, are the exception. They’re the only primates that operate under a matriarchal society, where the females are in charge.
