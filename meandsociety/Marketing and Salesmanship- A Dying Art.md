Why I Write.

I write music because it’s the only thing that keeps my mind from unraveling. If I could’ve chosen another path, I would have. But this isn’t about fame or attention—I hate being in the spotlight. For most of my life, I’ve been terrified of people. Not in the literal sense, but I’ve always expected rejection and disinterest. That’s what childhood trauma, especially at the hands of my dad, ingrains in you.

It’s strange to reflect on how that shaped me. I’ve spent years as a quiet observer, watching closely and noticing more than people think I do. Those observations have led me to realizations that feel revolutionary, at least to me. But I still carry my defects—some I’ve held onto tightly for over 30 years.

The Subconscious Opponent.

The battle I’m fighting now is with my own subconscious. It’s an opponent I can’t afford to underestimate. I’ve tried bulldozing through it in the past, only to find myself back where I started. Now, I’m learning to approach it differently—with caution, respect, and patience.

Because in the end, isn’t that what growth is? A careful unraveling of the patterns that have held us back, replacing them with something more true, more aligned with who we want to become.
