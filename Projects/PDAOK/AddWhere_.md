1\. Orient.

Let your eyes land on something ordinary—a texture, a shadow, a color.

Name it silently: “this, here.”

This phrase tells the nervous system the search is over for a moment.

2\. Weight.

Notice where the body meets gravity.

Without adjusting posture, feel the contact points—feet, seat, back.

Instead of holding yourself up, let the ground or chair hold you.

A small exhale often arrives on its own.

3\. Range.

Shift your attention from the one object outward until you sense the room’s edges.

Then inward again to one small detail.

That expansion–contraction is the body’s natural pulse of regulation.

4\. Breath without correction.

Don’t deepen it—just notice when it turns around.

That tiny pause at the top and bottom of each breath is the physical signature of equilibrium.

Stay with one or two of those pauses.

5\. Sensing the tone.

Ask quietly: If equilibrium had a temperature, what would it be right now?

Warm, cool, neutral—no need for words beyond that.

This names the quality of the moment without analysis.
