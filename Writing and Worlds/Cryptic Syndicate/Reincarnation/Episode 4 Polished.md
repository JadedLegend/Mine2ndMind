Episode 4

First Contact

Michael crossed the threshold. Something inside him clicked into place—not mechanical, but organic. Like a radio finally finding its frequency after years of static, or a key discovering it had always fit this particular lock.

Coherent resonance—that's what Brayne would call it later. But in the moment, Michael just felt his internal state become the strongest voice in his head, louder than doubt, louder than analysis, pushing his mind and body toward something that felt suspiciously like trust.

Still, a corner of his brain refused to surrender. That skeptical part of him kept running calculations like a computer trying to process impossible equations: Impossibility 1: Interdimensional travel. Impossibility 2: Meeting my own pseudonym. Impossibility 3: Feeling better than I have in years.

The math didn't add up. But his nervous system had already voted, and it was a landslide in favor of whatever this is, keep going.

His body knew something his mind hadn't figured out yet.

The Enhanced

Michael followed Brayne almost mindlessly through corridors lined with people who looked like they'd stepped out of a transhumanist fever dream—except the dream was lucid, and they were awake.

Some had crystalline enhancements growing from their temples—not implants exactly, more like their skulls had decided to sprout gemstones that caught and refracted thoughts instead of light. Others moved with the fluid grace of dancers who'd figured out how to bend physics, their steps leaving brief ripples in the air. One woman sat in lotus position three feet off the ground, meditating with the casual confidence of someone who'd forgotten gravity existed.

Michael stared, caught between awe and envy. How do you get to that level? he wondered. And what would it cost?

Each person seemed absorbed in their own self-improvement modality. Yoga that involved folding through dimensions—not metaphorically, but actually disappearing briefly into angles that shouldn't exist. Meditation that looked like controlled epilepsy, their bodies trembling with frequencies Michael could almost hear. Martial arts practiced against invisible opponents who might have been fighting back, judging by the way their practitioners dodged attacks from empty air.

It's like a spiritual gym, Michael thought, if spiritual gyms were designed by people who'd never heard the word 'impossible' and didn't want to start learning now.

The Mantra

Brayne's clairsentience picked up Michael's vulnerability like a barometer sensing an incoming storm. But his intention felt benevolent—not the predatory compassion of someone looking for weakness to exploit, but the steady presence of a therapist who genuinely wants to help rather than just collect fees.

"I have a grounding mantra I'd like you to experiment with," Brayne said, his voice carrying the authority of someone who'd guided others through this particular brand of reality adjustment before. "Follow me and listen to 'Return.'"

They found a quiet corner away from the impossible athletics. Brayne's voice shifted into something deeper, more rhythmic. Not quite singing, not quite speaking. Like a meditation teacher who'd learned to make words into medicine, syllables into surgery for the soul:

\---

Return

Breathe in.

Let it rise.

The hum is not noise—

It is memory, unwinding.

Breathe out.

Let it fall.

The weight you carried

Was never yours at all.

Feet on ground.

Mind in sky.

You are the still point

As the wires pass by.

Thoughts flicker—let them.

Sounds echo—bless them.

You are the observer

Between pulse and pattern.

You do not have to fix.

You only have to feel.

Each breath is a signal:

You're allowed to heal.

\---

The Click

Something extraordinary happened as the words settled into Michael's nervous system like seeds finding fertile soil.

Dissonance clicked into harmony. Like a massive, cosmic tuning fork had been struck somewhere in his chest, and suddenly every frequency in his body remembered how to sing the same song. The discord that had been his constant companion—that background static of existing wrong in the world—simply... resolved.

His capacity for holding paradox expanded—not gradually, but exponentially, like a computer suddenly upgraded with infinite processing power. The part of him that needed everything to make logical sense relaxed its death grip on reality. The white-knuckled tension he'd carried in his mind for decades finally unclenched. The part of him that had been fighting the impossibility of his situation finally agreed to a ceasefire.

This is happening, he realized, and the thought felt clean, uncomplicated. I don't need to understand it. I just need to not break it.

Brayne had primed him to coherently hold the totality of his situation: interdimensional refugee, diabetic mystic, writer whose fictional characters had turned out to be documentary subjects from parallel realities. The impossibilities didn't cancel each other out—they harmonized.

The next challenge would be integration—figuring out how to take action in this new reality without cracking the underlying fundamentals that held it all together. Learning to move without breaking the spell, to act without shattering the frequency.

Some kinds of healing, Michael realized, happen not when you figure everything out, but when you finally stop trying to figure everything out.
