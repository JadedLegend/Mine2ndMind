The Ballad of the McGriddle

Oh, ye child of divinity, born in the blessed Elysian Fields,

Where gods themselves dare not tread, lest they bow before thee.

The McGriddle: an alchemical masterpiece,

An edible symphony, a harmonious collision of elements divine.

Lo, the One True God, with incantations untold,

Summons forth this marvel of culinary sorcery.

He plucks it from trees kissed by ambrosia,

Wrapped in the morning dew of celestial pancakes.

Transported by the will of the cosmos to the humble halls of McDonald's,

Where a wayward cook—rescued from the throes of despair—

Cradles it in trembling hands,

Anointed by destiny to deliver unto us this treasure.

And there it lies, in thy mortal grasp,

A marvel your unworthy taste buds scarcely deserve.

Eggs, golden as the morning sun.

Bacon, crisp as the resolve of ancient kings.

But lo, they dared to dream greater,

They dared to crown it with cheese,

Binding the savory trinity together in perfect union.

And then, the pièce de résistance,

A pancake bun, soft as a lover’s whisper,

Infused with the nectar of the heavens themselves.

But wait! What light through yonder taste bud breaks?

It is the syrup nugget!

A confectionery grenade, exploding with unrestrained grandeur,

A burst of sweetness so profound,

It leaves your palate kneeling, begging for mercy,

Begging for one more bite.

Thus, the McGriddle reigns,

Not as mere sustenance,

But as a relic of the gods, a chalice of divine ecstasy,

Wrapped in cellophane and carried forth by the winds of fortune,

To your eager hands and grateful soul.

Praise be to thee, McGriddle.

You are more than food.

You are legend.
